Start here — the one thing that confused us

Bottom line: A Plaid client_id + secret read nothing on their own. They prove who you are; they do not grant access to anyone's accounts. Plaid has no phone-book of your banks, no "list all my accounts" endpoint, and no way to reuse a connection you made anywhere else. The only way your app ever sees a bank is if the account owner authenticates to that specific bank inside your app's own Plaid Link flow. Everything that feels convoluted about Plaid flows from this one fact.

The analogy that ends the confusion. Think of Plaid like a valet booth, not a key directory:

  • client_id + secret = your dealership license. It proves you're a legitimate operator and opens no car. By itself it reads zero account data. (plaid.com/docs/api/items · first-hand: "Your client_id and secret alone cannot retrieve account data—you must pair them with an access_token obtained from a specific Item.")
  • An Item = a valet key to exactly one car (one bank login), handed to you only after the owner unlocked that car in your booth. One Item covers all accounts behind that one login (checking + savings under one bank). (plaid.com/docs/quickstart/glossary · first-hand)
  • Link = the booth itself — the only place a key is ever cut. There is no master key that lists every car a person owns; you hold exactly the keys (Items) the operator personally created in your booth. (plaid.com/docs/link · first-hand)

The closest software analogy is "Sign in with Google": the consent is per-app and per-resource. Logging into Google somewhere else doesn't hand your app a read of the user's account. Plaid is the same — a connection made elsewhere does not transfer.

The trap most people fall into. Three things look like they should give an app "a view of my finances," and none of them do:

  • "I have my client_id and secret — why is the cache empty?" Because credentials are an identity, not an access grant. You still have to run a Link flow per bank.
  • "I connected my banks at my.plaid.com — why doesn't the gateway see them?" Because my.plaid.com is the consumer portal where individuals manage their own connections; it is a different account type from your developer dashboard, and its connections live in a different world from your app's Items. (plaid.com/docs/account · first-hand: "A Plaid developer account is different from a Plaid account at my.plaid.com." The access-denial half is a direct consequence of the Item model, not a verbatim line — phrase operator copy as the mechanism, not as a Plaid quote.)
  • "Doesn't Plaid Layer / phone-number recognition let me skip all this?" No. Layer just speeds up a returning user — it still mints a brand-new Item through your Link, gives no backend access to connections made elsewhere, and is incompatible with Hosted Link (which is the mode this gateway uses). (plaid.com/docs/layer · first-hand: "Layer is not compatible with the Hosted Link integration mode"; "establishes fresh connections within your application environment rather than providing backend access to existing external connections.")

If you came from SimpleFIN, where you paste one access URL and immediately see every bank behind the Bridge, this is the jarring difference: Plaid is per-bank, by effort, with a per-bank login each time. That is not a bug or a misconfiguration — it is the model. The rest of this report walks the consequences honestly.

The access model — Items, access_tokens, and why there's no "list my accounts"

Bottom line: the entire hierarchy is access_token (1) → Item (1 bank login) → accounts (N). Every read endpoint is keyed by an access_token, and an access_token is only ever born from a Link-minted Item. Coverage = exactly the set of banks the operator personally linked. Nothing more.

Must-remember: a person with accounts at 4 banks = 4 separate Link auths = 4 Items = 4 vaulted access_tokens. There is genuinely no Item-spanning handle and no endpoint to enumerate the Items behind a credential — so your database is the only source of truth for "what did I connect." Lose that mapping and you cannot ask Plaid to recover it.

This is architecturally fine for us — it maps onto "one credential fans out to N connectors," just like one Google credential fans out to contacts+calendar. The difference is that with Plaid each fan-out leaf is an independently-authenticated Item, each its own credential/connector row keyed by external_item_id. The "no list-all-accounts endpoint" claim is a structural inference from the access model (every read is keyed by an access_token), not a quotable line — but it follows necessarily and is corroborated by the absence of any such endpoint in the API. (plaid.com/docs/api/items · first-hand; negative claim is model-inference)

The only Plaid calls that work with just client_id + secret are /institutions/get and /institutions/get_by_id — and they return institution metadata (names, logos, supported products), never account data. They require a country_codes param. Useful for logos; useless for reading money. (plaid.com/docs/api/institutions · first-hand)

Link flows & token lifecycle — the stacked expiry windows

Bottom line: the only durable artifact is the access_token; everything upstream of it is short-lived, and our loopback/no-webhook design stacks three short windows in a row. Get them wrong and the link is silently lost.

The capture pipeline (Hosted Link, our path)

  1. /link/token/create with hosted_link: {} → returns a hosted_link_url. An empty object is enough; no account-manager enablement and no Dashboard redirect-URI registration for web Hosted Link. (plaid.com/docs/link/hosted-link · first-hand)
  2. Operator opens the URL, authenticates at the bank inside Plaid's hosted pages.
  3. We poll /link/token/get (no webhook) to retrieve the public_token(s).
  4. Exchange each public_token via /item/public_token/exchange → a permanent access_token + item_id, sealed in the vault.

Three short windows, in series:

  • Hosted Link URL defaults to 30 minutes when Plaid isn't delivering it (SMS = 24h, email = 7d). We mint the URL server-side and the operator opens it themselves, so we land in the 30-minute bucket by default. You must set hosted_link.url_lifetime_seconds explicitly (max 21 days). (plaid.com/docs/link/hosted-link · first-hand)
  • /link/token/get serves the token for only 6 hours after session completion. If the gateway is down for >6h after the operator links, the Item is lost. (plaid.com/docs/link/hosted-link · first-hand)
  • The public_token itself dies 30 minutes after issue and is single-use. Exchange immediately on first sighting — don't queue it. (plaid.com/docs/quickstart/glossary · first-hand)

Multi-Item Link changes the response shape

With enable_multi_item_link: true, the operator can add several banks in one session — but tokens are no longer delivered via onSuccess. They arrive as a results.item_add_results array from /link/token/get (or via webhook, which we don't use). The doc is explicit: "The on_success object from /link/token/get is deprecated for this flow, as it contains only one token instead of all public tokens." (plaid.com/docs/link/multi-item-link · first-hand)

Must-remember: a poller that reads on_success (single token) will silently capture only one of N linked banks. The poller must iterate the item_add_results array and exchange each entry into its own access_token + external_item_id.

completion_redirect_uri is not a success signal

If you wire a loopback completion_redirect_uri for UX, note Plaid calls it "whether the user successfully completed the session, or asked Plaid to exit." So even with a redirect, you must still poll /link/token/get to confirm an Item was actually created — treating the redirect as "done" would create phantom/empty connectors. (plaid.com/docs/link/hosted-link · first-hand)

OAuth banks: registration is mostly not our problem (web Hosted Link)

The OAuth guide's redirect-URI / Android-package / iOS-universal-link registration steps are for client-side embedded Link. Web Hosted Link opened in a desktop browser needs no Dashboard redirect-URI registration — only mobile-app Hosted Link (a universal link) does. Keep the gateway on web Hosted Link and this whole class of setup disappears. (plaid.com/docs/link/oauth · first-hand; the Hosted-Link exemption for OAuth banks specifically was not re-confirmed on that page — flagged in Open Questions.)

The Item lifecycle, re-auth & consent expiry — the convoluted core

Bottom line: connections break and need a human to re-link them, on a recurring ~annual cadence, even when nothing is wrong. This is the single biggest gap between Plaid's reality and the "connect once, faithful cache" mantra — and it is exactly what SimpleFIN's static access URL does not impose. If you build the Plaid connector and stop at "read-only and done," your finance cache will silently rot within a year.

Re-auth is a permanent operational cadence, not a one-time setup. Under CFPB Section 1033, consumers must reauthorize roughly every 12 months. consent_expiration_time is a real field on the Item, readable via /item/get. Capital One ("consent refreshed after one year"), Fidelity ("every 12 months"), and Bank of America's new API all set ~annual expiry. A perfectly healthy, untouched connection will expire on a rolling annual basis and require a human to re-run Link. (plaid.com/docs/api/items · first-hand for the field; per-bank durations from plaid.com/docs/link/oauth + search snippets)

How a connection breaks

  • ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED — the central recoverable failure: "the login details of this item have changed (credentials, MFA, or required user action) and a user login is required." The only fix is Link update mode; there is no server-side recovery. (plaid.com/docs/errors/item · first-hand)
  • Consent expiry — the ~annual 1033 clock above.
  • USER_PERMISSION_REVOKED / USER_ACCOUNT_REVOKED (PNC, per-account) — the user revoked access; the webhook "may not fire if revocation occurred directly at institution," so a bank-side revoke can silently kill sync. (plaid.com/docs/errors/item · first-hand)
  • ITEM_NOT_FOUND / INSTITUTION_NO_LONGER_SUPPORTED — unrecoverable; requires a brand-new Item (new external_item_id).

A broken Item makes /transactions/sync go QUIET, not loud. It returns empty arrays — indistinguishable from "no new transactions." A cache that only watches sync output will report a dead connection as merely idle. The authoritative health signal is a separate /item/get poll: its nullable error object surfaces ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED, and status.transactions.last_successful_update / last_failed_update + consent_expiration_time tell you freshness and the consent clock. This is a second call path the current sync-only connector does not have. (plaid.com/docs/api/items + plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions · first-hand)

The good news: update mode is cheap to recover from

Update mode does NOT mint a new access_token and needs no exchange. You create a link_token carrying the existing access_token (30-min expiry in update mode), the user re-auths in Link, and the same access_token resumes. Crucially for us: access_token and external_item_id are unchanged across re-auth, so no domain rows get repointed — just clear the error flag and resume the cursor (it backfills the missed window automatically). (plaid.com/docs/link/update-mode · first-hand)

The proactive warnings we forfeit

Plaid normally pushes 7-day advance warnings — PENDING_DISCONNECT (US/CA, with disconnect_time + reason) and PENDING_EXPIRATION (EU/UK). These are webhook-only. Our loopback gateway has no receiver, so:

  • Consent-expiry breaks are recoverable proactively — we can poll consent_expiration_time on a cadence and compute our own "expiring soon" warning. This covers the common annual re-consent case.
  • Migration-driven disconnects (US/CA) are not — disconnect_time is webhook-only, so we only learn after sync fails. Acceptable to detect reactively. (plaid.com/docs/api/items · first-hand; absence of pollable disconnect_time inferred from the /item/get field list)

Don't treat every error as "re-auth needed." INSTITUTION_DOWN / INSTITUTION_NOT_RESPONDING / INSTITUTION_NOT_AVAILABLE are transient — back off and retry, do not prompt the operator. Lumping them with ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED produces false re-auth prompts that erode trust. The connector needs a real state machine (see codebase gaps). (plaid.com/docs/errors/institution · first-hand)

One more lifecycle landmine: an Item with update_type = user_present_required cannot refresh in the background at all — it always needs a user-present re-Link. Surface this per-connection so a stale last_synced_at on such a connection isn't mistaken for a bug. (plaid.com/docs/api/items · first-hand; confidence medium)

Data & sync — cursor semantics, pending→posted, and the empty first call

Bottom line: /transactions/sync is the right primitive and maps cleanly onto a faithful cache, but it has three sharp edges: the first call is usually empty, pending→posted is a remove+add (not a modify), and the cursor must be committed atomically or the cache silently diverges.

Cursor mechanics

You poll with your saved cursor; the response returns added / modified / removed arrays plus has_more + next_cursor. Omitting the cursor returns full history. The cursor is valid for ≥1 year. (plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions · first-hand)

Never persist next_cursor mid-pagination. On TRANSACTIONS_SYNC_MUTATION_DURING_PAGINATION, "the entire pagination request loop must be restarted beginning with the cursor for the first page." Buffer all pages and commit the final next_cursor only after has_more = false, in the same DB transaction as the applied rows. The cursor is the only read-side idempotency mechanism — there is no idempotency-key header for reads. (plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions + plaid.com/docs/api · first-hand)

pending → posted is remove + add with a NEW transaction_id, not an in-place modify. When a pending transaction posts, the pending id shows up in removed and the posted transaction arrives fresh in added (carrying the old id in pending_transaction_id). If the connector keys only on transaction_id and ignores removed, every posted transaction double-counts its pending ghost. This is exactly what tombstones (ADR 0004) are for. (plaid.com/docs/transactions/transactions-data · first-hand)

The empty first call

Initial sync is asynchronous and two-phase. "It is common that no transactions will be returned during this first call." A transactions_update_status field walks NOT_READY → INITIAL_UPDATE_COMPLETE (~30 days) → HISTORICAL_UPDATE_COMPLETE (full window). With no webhook, we learn data is ready only by re-polling. The Console must render "syncing / not ready" distinct from "connected but empty," or the operator thinks it's broken right after linking. Kick an immediate follow-up sync after exchange rather than waiting the full 6h interval. (plaid.com/docs/transactions + plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions · first-hand)

History depth is frozen at link time

transactions.days_requested (default 90, range 1–730) is set when the link_token is minted and is immutable after the Item initializes — "This field has no effect if Transactions is already initialized on the Item." You cannot widen history later without re-linking the bank. For a faithful financial-history cache, request the deep window up front. (Our connector currently sets 365 for SimpleFIN parity — see codebase gaps; research recommends 730.) (plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions · first-hand)

Balances and sign convention

  • Use /accounts/get for cached balances — free, returns Plaid's last-known balance, "typically update daily" while Transactions runs, 15/min per Item. Accounts already arrive inside each /transactions/sync page too. (plaid.com/docs/api/accounts · first-hand)
  • Reserve /accounts/balance/get for explicit live drills — it forces a real-time pull (p50 ~3s, p95 ~11s), is billed per successful call, and is capped at 5/min, 30/HOUR per Item. A chatty agent on this path is a self-inflicted rate-limit and cost footgun. (plaid.com/docs/balance + plaid.com/docs/errors/rate-limit-exceeded · first-hand)
  • Plaid's amount is positive = money OUT — the opposite of our convention (negative = out). The connector negates on write; this must stay covered by a fixture test or spending/income silently flips for Plaid only. (plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions · first-hand)

Enrichment is free in the sync output

PFC category, merchant_name, logo_url, website, payment_channel, and the counterparties[] array all arrive inside /transactions/sync for Plaid-sourced Items — no extra call. The standalone /transactions/enrich endpoint is a separate, separately-billed product for enriching your own external transactions; do not wire it. Vendor the PFC taxonomy CSV (plaid.com/documents/pfc-taxonomy-all.csv) rather than hardcoding categories, so SimpleFIN's agent-inferred categories share the same vocabulary. (plaid.com/docs/api/products/enrich · first-hand; the commonly-cited ~16 primary-category count is general knowledge, not first-hand-verified here)

Webhooks vs our polling — what loopback loses, and what it gains

Bottom line: polling loses no transaction data and is explicitly a supported pattern. It loses push latency (bounded by the bank anyway) and the proactive 7-day disconnect warning. In exchange it deletes an entire security-critical surface. For a loopback self-host gateway, poll-only is the correct call — confirmed, not a compromise.

Why we lose almost nothing on freshness: "Plaid typically checks for new transactions data between one and four times per day, depending on the institution." A webhook can't make data fresher than the bank's own 1–4x/day cadence — it only tells you the moment one of those refreshes landed. Our 6h poll (4x/day) lines up with the upper end. Honest lag: up to ~6h, often dominated by the bank's own delay. /transactions/refresh (2/min, 120/hour per Item) covers on-demand "sync now." (plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions · first-hand)

Webhooks aren't even reliable on their own. Plaid retries for 24h with exponential backoff, 10s timeout, and explicitly delivers duplicates and out-of-order — so "design your application to handle duplicate and out-of-order webhooks." Every robust integration is told to build a polling reconciliation fallback regardless. We are simply running that fallback path; we just skip the low-latency nudge. (plaid.com/docs/api/webhooks · first-hand)

Skipping the receiver is a security win, not a regression. Trusting a Plaid webhook requires a 14-step ES256/JWS verification: read the Plaid-Verification JWS header, assert alg == ES256, fetch the JWK via /webhook_verification_key/get, cache it and honor rotation, verify the signature, check iat within 5 minutes (replay guard), then SHA-256 the exact raw body and constant-time-compare it to the claim. Skip any step and an attacker can forge webhooks. Plus the URL must be public HTTPS with a valid cert — fundamentally incompatible with loopback. Poll-only deletes this whole class of work and risk. (plaid.com/docs/api/webhooks/webhook-verification · first-hand)

What we genuinely forgo: the proactive 7-day PENDING_DISCONNECT (US/CA) warning, which has no clean polling equivalent. We recover the consent-expiry class by polling consent_expiration_time, and detect everything else reactively at the next poll. The correct substitute for the receiver is an /item/get health poll, not a webhook ingress — see the next section and codebase gaps.

Do not set the webhook param in /link/token/create. Omitting it makes the Item webhook-free with no Dashboard registration and no error. (Whether /item/webhook/update exists to retrofit a webhook later is unverified — it was not present in the fetched docs. Don't claim webhooks can be added later without checking.) (plaid.com/docs/api/webhooks · first-hand; /item/webhook/update existence UNVERIFIED)

Environments, plans & cost — opaque, and hostile to a long-lived cache

Bottom line: there are only two environments (Sandbox = fake data, Production = real). Plaid publishes no dollar amounts anywhere — pricing only appears on the last page of a Production request form. The free Trial tier is genuinely free real data, but it's narrow, lifetime-capped at 10 Items, and cliff-edged into opaque billing. And the Transactions billing model — a per-Item monthly subscription that accrues "as long as a valid access_token exists" — is precisely the worst case for a cache that deliberately holds long-lived tokens.

The 10-Item Trial cap is a lifetime counter. "Removing Items created on a Trial plan (using /item/remove) will not allow you to create more Items." Every re-link, every wrong-bank test, every MFA failure that still created an Item permanently burns one of 10 free slots. Steer dogfood/test links to Sandbox to avoid bleeding the free tier. (plaid.com/docs/account/billing · first-hand)

Trial → paid is a cliff, and it's retroactive. On upgrade you "only retain Production access to any products you explicitly requested," and "If you added a subscription fee product (such as ... Transactions) to an Item during your trial, upon upgrading ... you will begin to be charged for that subscription." So exceeding 10 Items flips every existing Transactions Item to a billed monthly subscription at once — and if you forgot to list Transactions on the request form, you can lose access to it. (plaid.com/docs/account/billing · first-hand)

Our cache architecture is the billing worst case on paid Production. We deliberately retain a valid access_token per Item indefinitely and poll every 6h. The Transactions subscription bills on token existence, not call volume — so every connected bank is a perpetual monthly charge regardless of how often the agent reads. Disconnect/erase MUST call /item/remove to stop billing; deleting local rows alone leaves a billable zombie Item alive. (plaid.com/docs/account/billing + plaid.com/docs/api/items · first-hand)

The dollar amounts themselves are unobtainable first-hand — gated behind the Production request form. An operator cannot cost-compare Plaid vs free SimpleFIN without starting a Production application. The honest framing for our docs: "free up to 10 Items on a new US/CA Trial; beyond that, paid Production with per-Item monthly Transactions subscriptions + per-call Balance fees, priced only inside Plaid's request form." Development environment was decommissioned 2024-06-20, so there is no free real-data middle tier. (plaid.com/docs/quickstart/glossary · first-hand)

Compliance & OAuth registration — the Trial exemption (and its asterisks)

Bottom line: for an eligible operator the compliance burden collapses to near-zero — Trial defers MSA, security questionnaire, and formal company/app info. But two asterisks bite anyway: some OAuth banks block apps with an empty Application Display Info profile regardless of Trial, and certain institutions (Schwab especially) have out-of-band, multi-week access requests the Console cannot automate.

The OAuth Production prerequisites are five items — Production access, Dashboard registration, Application Display Info + Company Info, the MSA (US/CA), and the security questionnaire (US/CA) — and the page states: "If you are on a Trial plan, you do not need to complete these requirements until you upgrade to a paid plan." (plaid.com/docs/link/oauth · first-hand)

The bank-side gate Trial does NOT waive: Application Display Info "is public information that end users will see ... during OAuth flows," and "some US institutions ... will not allow apps with an empty profile to access their OAuth implementations." This is enforced by the bank, not Plaid — so it remains a required onboarding step for OAuth banks even on Trial. A self-hoster who skips the profile will silently fail to connect specific banks. (plaid.com/docs/link/oauth · first-hand)

How it maps to OUR codebase — and the spec GAPS

Bottom line: the connector's sync half is already correct and matches the docs. The onboarding half is specced (docs/specs/plaid-hosted-link.md, dated today) but not yet merged — its migration 0006_plaid_app.sql does not exist yet (migrations stop at 0005). The two pieces the spec's "read-only and done" framing hides — Item-health polling and an update-mode re-link UX — are genuine V0-needs that are currently out of scope.

What's already right in /src/connectors/plaid.ts (422 lines)

  • Cursor drain — accumulates added+modified (deduped, modified-wins) across has_more pages and captures the final next_cursor as the durable checkpoint (lines ~321–340).
  • Tombstones the removed array — per-id last-wins reconciliation; surviving removed ids yield deletion records the runner tombstones per ADR 0004 (lines ~304–306, 359–361). This correctly handles pending→posted double-counting.
  • amount negation — negateToString() at write (lines ~264–265, 411), with the convention documented in the file header.
  • Sandbox path — /sandbox/public_token/create for headless CI of the exchange+sync path (line ~248).

What the spec (docs/specs/plaid-hosted-link.md) covers but hasn't landed

  • enable_multi_item_link: true + hosted_link: { url_lifetime_seconds: 10800 } (3h, explicit — correctly avoids the 30-min default).
  • Per-Item external_item_id keying: credential conflict target (user_id, provider, external_item_id), connector conflict target (credential_id, external_item_id) — the non-negotiable fix without which the second bank overwrites the first.
  • Server-side /link/token/get polling with idempotent re-exchange (catch already-consumed → {skipped:'already_exchanged'}).
  • A plaid_app store mirroring the BYO-app pattern; the 0006_plaid_app.sql migration + db/schema.sql mirror.

GAP 1 — no Item-health visibility. The connector's itemGet() (line ~237) calls /item/get but only extracts institutionId. It does not read the error object, status.transactions.last_*_update, consent_expiration_time, or update_type. So a broken Item is invisible — sync just goes quiet. This is the single most important missing piece for honesty of last_synced_at (mantra 2). Needs a periodic /item/get health poll that stores error_code + consent_expiration_time + update_type per connection and surfaces a per-connection state into describe_context and the Console.

GAP 2 — no update-mode re-link flow. The spec lists the manual-token fallback as a non-goal and does not include a Console route to re-launch Link against a broken item_id. Because ~annual re-consent is guaranteed (Capital One / Fidelity / BoA), this is not power-user scope — without it every connection silently rots within a year. It's cheap to build: the access_token / external_item_id survive re-auth, so no row repointing — mint a link_token with the stored access_token, re-open Hosted Link, clear the error flag, resume the cursor.

GAP 3 — /item/remove not wired into disconnect/erase. On paid Production a disconnect that only deletes local rows leaves a billable zombie Item. The data-control plane's disconnect/erase must dispatch /item/remove OUT to Plaid (an operator-initiated lifecycle action — distinct from the agent action surface, which stays actions: []).

GAP 4 (minor) — history depth. The connector sets transactions: { days_requested: 365 } (line ~195) for SimpleFIN parity. That satisfies the "≥180" floor, but since the value is frozen at Item init and the cache is meant to be a faithful financial history, research recommends 730. Worth a deliberate decision before this becomes immutable per Item.

GAP 5 — error-code state machine. The connector surfaces a non-sensitive error_code (line ~171) but does not branch on it. It needs: action_required (ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED, ACCESS_NOT_GRANTED, USER_PERMISSION_REVOKED, consent-expiring-soon) → prompt re-link; transient (INSTITUTION_DOWN/NOT_RESPONDING/NOT_AVAILABLE) → backoff; dead (ITEM_NOT_FOUND, INSTITUTION_NO_LONGER_SUPPORTED) → fresh Link; and crucially terminal 429s (TRIAL_CONNECTION_LIMIT, CREDITS_EXHAUSTED) must NOT be retried — backoff can never clear a hard quota.

Operational gotchas — the short list to tape to the wall

Bottom line: most of these are one-line traps that cause a "Plaid is broken" panic when they're actually expected behavior. Bake them into Console copy and the connector's error handling.

Empty cache right after linking is normal. First /transactions/sync commonly returns nothing; backfill lands over subsequent syncs. Show "syncing / not ready," kick an immediate re-poll, never render empty-but-connected as failure.

Trial 10-Item cap surfaces as a 429. TRIAL_CONNECTION_LIMIT / CREDITS_EXHAUSTED arrive in the same RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED family as transient sync throttles. Branch on error_code, treat these as terminal, surface to the operator — do not retry. (plaid.com/docs/errors/rate-limit-exceeded · first-hand)

Live-balance is a footgun. /accounts/balance/get is 30/HOUR per Item and billed per call. A chatty agent will exhaust it and run up cost. Route normal balance reads to free cached /accounts/get; gate /accounts/balance/get behind throttled fetch_live only.

Sandbox green ≠ Production green. Sandbox uses a single generic OAuth flow (no per-bank behavior), skips consent checkboxes, allows http redirects, and its data is internally inconsistent. It cannot validate Chase/PNC OAuth quirks or balance/transaction reconciliation. Gate release on a real Production Trial smoke against a real OAuth bank. (plaid.com/docs/sandbox · first-hand)

Test the break→recover loop in Sandbox. Force ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED with /sandbox/item/reset_login (Sandbox Items also auto-expire into it at 30 days), force credential errors via the password field (error_INVALID_CREDENTIALS, mfa_device + code 1234), and validate the full detect→update-mode→resume path before claiming dogfood-ready. Sandbox webhook tooling is useless to us (no receiver). (plaid.com/docs/sandbox + plaid.com/docs/sandbox/test-credentials · first-hand)

Vault design already matches Plaid's expectations. Long-lived access_token sealed, never client-side; app secret vault-sealed. A compromised access_token rotates via /item/access_token/invalidate (returns a NEW token for the same Item — re-seal in place, no re-link). This is distinct from the re-link required for ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED. (plaid.com/docs/api · first-hand for headers; invalidate is snippet-level)

/item/remove is immediate and destructive. No grace period — the token invalidates at once and any in-flight sync fails. Define cleanup semantics in disconnect (orphan vs hard-delete) and don't call it mid-sync.

Recommendation & phasing

Bottom line: keep SimpleFIN as the default, free, zero-friction finance connector; ship Plaid honestly as the coverage-upgrade / hosted-tier provider that carries real recurring cost and recurring human toil. Plaid is more convoluted than it first looks — but the convolution is bounded and concentrated in two named units (a health poll + an update-mode re-link). With those, it's a sound second provider.

The honest SimpleFIN vs Plaid trade:

  • SimpleFIN — one static access URL covering every bank behind the Bridge; no per-bank Link, no consent expiry, no per-Item billing, no re-auth cadence. Structurally ideal for a single operator's faithful cache.
  • Plaid — breadth-by-effort (one Link auth per bank), an opaque per-Item monthly subscription on paid Production, and an unavoidable ~annual re-consent cliff that makes a long-lived cache silently rot unless we build health-polling + an update-mode UX. Genuinely free for an eligible operator (new US/CA team ≥2026-04-15, ≤10 lifetime Items, ~60s auto-approval) — but that tier is narrow and cliff-edged.

Phasing

  1. Land the Hosted Link onboarding (the current spec). Per-Item external_item_id schema (0006_plaid_app.sql) + multi-item array-aware /link/token/get capture + explicit url_lifetime_seconds. Reconsider days_requested (365 vs 730) before it's frozen per Item.
  2. Add the two pieces the "read-only" framing hides (own wave). (a) An /item/get health poll feeding an honest per-connection status into describe_context and the Console; (b) a Console update-mode re-link flow — cheap, since access_token/external_item_id survive re-auth.
  3. Wire /item/remove into disconnect/erase to stop billing.
  4. Default balances to free /accounts/get; gate /accounts/balance/get behind throttled fetch_live.
  5. Keep the loopback no-webhook design — confirmed correct and a security simplification; accept bounded ~6h freshness (capped by the bank's own 1–4x/day) and reactive break detection.
  6. Gate release on a real Production Trial smoke against a real OAuth bank — Sandbox cannot validate institution-specific behavior.

What's safe to skip in V0: a webhook receiver (deletes the ES256/JWS surface), Plaid Layer (incompatible with Hosted Link), the real-time Balance product as a default, /transactions/enrich (PFC already in sync output), multi-item staggered-polling (single-tenant won't hit limits), LEI handling (stayed to ≥July 2026), an embedded Link widget, and the proactive US/CA PENDING_DISCONNECT warning (detect those reactively). Console copy must lead with: "Plaid only reads banks you link inside this gateway's Link flow — your my.plaid.com connections do not transfer."

Open questions & what to live-verify

Bottom line: the access model, lifecycle, and sync semantics are first-hand-confirmed. The residual uncertainties are mostly billing dollar-figures (unobtainable without a Production application) and a handful of edge behaviors worth a Sandbox/Production smoke before claiming dogfood-ready.

  • Dollar amounts for the Transactions monthly per-Item subscription and the per-call Balance fee — unobtainable first-hand; gated behind the Production request form. Docs publish only plan names and "one-time / subscription / per-request" structures.
  • Does merely polling /item/get (vs /transactions/sync) incur a per-call charge on paid teams, and is /accounts/get during sync separately metered? Billing nuance by plan is unverified — affects how aggressively we can health-poll.
  • Which Item-creation events increment the Trial 10-Item lifetime counter? Docs confirm /item/remove doesn't free a slot but don't say whether failed/abandoned Link sessions that still create an Item count. Steer test links to Sandbox as the safe hedge.
  • Is update-mode Hosted Link registration-free for OAuth banks the same way initial Hosted Link is? Confirmed for initial Hosted Link; the update-mode Hosted Link URL flow for OAuth banks was not re-confirmed first-hand.
  • Exact in-band shape of /transactions/sync on a broken Item (succeed-empty vs error in the body) — resolved in practice by the /item/get health poll, but worth a Sandbox /sandbox/item/reset_login test to validate the full break→detect→update-mode→resume loop.
  • Does a 429 carry a documented Retry-After / backoff contract? The rate-limit page gives per-window limits; an explicit Retry-After was not confirmed, so the connector's backoff is currently a guess.
  • update_type = user_present_required — is there any unattended refresh path, or only periodic user-present re-Link? Determines whether such banks are viable for a cadence-synced cache at all.
  • Per-institution consent-expiry durations beyond the confirmed Capital One (1yr) / Fidelity (12mo) / BoA-new-API (12mo), and whether non-OAuth credential-based Items ever get a non-null consent_expiration_time.
  • Does completion_redirect_uri to a loopback URL work under env=production without prior Dashboard registration? Polling is the safe fallback regardless, but verify before relying on auto-return.
  • Bank of America 2026 migration scope — exact timeline per region, and whether it touches Sandbox; the runbook needs specifics.
