# Trademark and brand policy

The **code** in this repository is free software under [AGPL-3.0-or-later](LICENSE). The **name and the mark** are not part of that grant.

## What this covers

- The name **Postern**.
- The **door mark** — the Postern logo and its variants, in every rendering that ships in this repository (including `docs/design/logo/`).

Copyright and trademark rights in these are held by Neelabh Kumar and are **not licensed** with the source. AGPL-3.0 grants rights over copyrightable software; it grants nothing over names and marks, and this file simply says so out loud so nobody has to guess.

## What you may do without asking

- **Say what your software is.** "Built on Postern", "a Postern connector", "compatible with Postern", "a fork of Postern" — accurate, descriptive statements of fact are always fine and always will be.
- **Run it, modify it, and distribute it** under the AGPL, including commercially. That is the whole point of the license.
- **Keep the name in place while you contribute back.** Patches, forks-for-a-pull-request, and personal builds do not need renaming.
- **Write about it.** Reviews, tutorials, talks, comparisons, screenshots.

## What needs a different name

**If you distribute a modified version, rename it.** Ship it under your own name and your own mark, and describe it as *based on* or *a fork of* Postern rather than *as* Postern. This is the ordinary open-source arrangement — Firefox/Iceweasel, Chromium/Chrome — and it exists for one reason: someone downloading a thing called "Postern" should be able to rely on it being this project's code and behaving the way this project's documentation says. A modified build under the same name breaks that, and the person it breaks it for is the user, not the maintainer.

Likewise, do not use the name or the mark:

- as the name of your product, service, company, or hosted offering;
- in a domain name, package name, app-store listing, or social handle, in a way that implies this project publishes or endorses it;
- as your own logo or favicon;
- in any way that suggests affiliation, sponsorship, or endorsement that does not exist.

## Asking

Anything not covered above, or a use you think is reasonable and this file seems to forbid: open an issue and ask. The intent here is narrow — prevent confusion about what is and is not this project — not to make the software awkward to use.
